development / Case study

Anthropos

Project Overview

Connecting housing awareness with routes to action. Anthropos is a New York City housing advocacy and resource website. Its homepage introduces the issue, presents a petition, and helps visitors find official contacts and district-lookup tools.

Project type — Civic information and advocacy website. Work shown — Content organization, responsive page design, petition interface, and resource navigation. Technology — HTML and CSS, Netlify form markup, and external official-service links and embeds. The repository is named HousingIsARight, and some page metadata uses HousingNYC.

The experience supports two intended journeys: understanding the issue and taking action, or moving directly toward a relevant housing-support resource. It does not itself provide legal services or determine eligibility for public assistance.

Visit Anthropos

The Problem

An advocacy site has to do more than explain an issue. It also needs to give visitors clear choices: learn more, support a campaign, contact representatives, or find help.

The design challenge was to separate those tasks while connecting them in a coherent experience. A petition, an official contact card, and a resource for someone facing housing instability require different information and next steps.

Public-service links, statistics, and elected-official information also need ongoing maintenance. The case study discusses their presentation and sourcing without treating the site’s historical content as current legal guidance or independently validated research.

Goals & Objectives

Connect explanation to action — Structure the homepage around the issue, petition, official contacts, and local district lookup.

Provide direct resource routes — Create recognizable destinations for eviction help, housing information, legal aid, shelters, benefits, charities, and volunteering.

Make information inspectable — Include dates and source links for the statistics page, and route visitors to the organizations providing services.

Support mobile reading and navigation — Collapse multi-column content, contain the long menu within the viewport, and label form controls and embedded tools.

Design Process

Information architecture — A broad Resources hub links to more specific topics, while the homepage groups advocacy tasks into sections. Direct navigation entries let visitors reach a topic without following the campaign narrative first.

Content hierarchy — The opening statement and explanatory copy establish the subject; the petition follows as a concrete action. Contact cards then provide official routes, and the district section helps visitors locate representatives for their own address.

Petition interface — The form groups first name, last name, email, and ZIP code with labels and autocomplete hints. Netlify form and spam-control markup, plus a thank-you destination, provide the integration structure. A rendered form does not establish signature storage or delivery to officials.

District lookup — The site embeds the NYC Council’s district tool and links to official state and federal lookup pages. This connects visitors with external tools rather than claiming a custom district-matching engine.

Readability and responsive behavior — Arsenal headings contrast with Cormorant Garamond body text. The CSS defines focus indicators, a skip link, responsive image candidates, and narrower-screen grid changes. The compact menu uses details/summary and a scrollable panel. These observations do not establish tested accessibility or successful use under urgent conditions.

Challenges & Iterations

Expanding the resource architecture — Revision 1dcd87e added dedicated pages for eviction, housing law, rent regulation, charities, legal aid, Section 8, shelters, and volunteering. It also reworked the homepage and resources hub. The resulting structure offers more specific destinations; its effect on successful help-seeking has not been measured.

Adjusting mobile density — Revision 779e8e3 changed overall interface scale and logo sizing and added a constrained, scrollable mobile menu. The source documents the implementation change, but it does not prove an accessibility improvement. Readability and zoom behavior remain useful areas for direct evaluation.

Keeping external tools reachable — The district section combines an embedded Council map with a separate link and additional official lookup destinations. Visitors have another route when the embed is unsuitable or unavailable.

Managing service and content boundaries — The petition, official contacts, and third-party resource links introduce dependencies beyond the page layout. Submission handling, delivery practices, privacy statements, and resource accuracy require separate verification; none were tested by sending personal information during this review.

Outcomes & Reflection

Delivered — A deployed civic website that brings explanatory content, a petition interface, official contact routes, external district tools, and housing-resource categories into one navigable structure.

The work highlights the distinction between making an action visible and verifying that it reaches its intended outcome. A complete civic-service journey also depends on current information, functioning third-party tools, understandable data handling, and confirmed submission operations.

No petition totals, policy changes, referral successes, or measurable usability improvements are claimed. Useful next steps would be content-owner review, end-to-end form verification with an agreed test process, and task-based feedback from the intended audience.
